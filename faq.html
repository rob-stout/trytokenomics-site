FAQ

The windows, the meters, and the caveats.

Tokenomics shows each provider's usage in that provider's own units — and providers don't agree on how to count. This page explains what the numbers mean, why some tabs show two meters and others one, and how sign-in and privacy work.

Reading your usage numbers

How do Claude's 5-Hour and 7-Day windows work?

These are Anthropic's own quota windows, not something Tokenomics invented. The 5-Hour Window is the rolling cap most people hit first — it resets about five hours after your first message in the current block. If Tokenomics shows 80% with two hours left, you're close to a rate limit, so pace your prompts or switch tasks.

The 7-Day Window is the longer ceiling, resetting weekly from your first message of the cycle. On Pro plans you'll also see an Opus sub-counter inside the 7-day window — Opus carries its own weekly cap on top of the shared total, so it can run dry while the 7-day window still has room.

Why does Anthropic show only one window when other providers show two?

Anthropic is the exception. Claude on the web, the Claude desktop app, and Claude Code all draw down the same 5-hour and 7-day windows on your subscription — one set of limits covers every surface, so the Anthropic tab needs only the 5-hour and 7-day split.

OpenAI and Google structure their products the other way: the same account has a separate meter per surface. That's why those brands render two bars instead of one — see the next answer.

Why does my ChatGPT tab show two progress bars?

ChatGPT and Codex CLI are two different products that share one OpenAI login but bill against separate limits. Consumer ChatGPT runs on its own 3-hour and weekly windows; Codex CLI has its own weekly quota tied to your subscription tier. Same account, different meters — and OpenAI doesn't return a combined view, so Tokenomics renders them as two bars to show which limit you're closer to.

The same is true for Gemini: gemini.google.com and the Gemini CLI share a Google account but track usage independently.

Why does my widget show OpenAI twice?

Widgets render each usage pool as its own row, because the compact format has no room to nest. If you've connected both ChatGPT and Codex CLI, the OpenAI brand contributes two rows. On the small widget you pick exactly one pool to show — open the widget's edit panel and choose ChatGPT or Codex CLI.

Upgrading from an earlier version? If your small widget was set to "OpenAI" or "Google AI," it now maps to Codex CLI or Gemini CLI respectively — those entries were already tracking the CLI pools, since chat readers for those brands didn't exist before. To show the chat pool instead, re-select it from the edit panel.

Accounts & privacy

Is my usage data sent anywhere?

No. Tokenomics reads usage from the AI tools you already use, saves only the usage numbers its widgets need on your Mac, and sends nothing to any server the author controls — no analytics, no telemetry, no tracking code inside the app or the browser extension. The full, auditable breakdown is on the Privacy page.

How does signing in work?

For every provider that ships a command-line tool, Tokenomics reads the credentials those tools already stored on your Mac — you never hand Tokenomics a password, and there's nothing to paste. The few providers without a CLI use an API key you paste once, kept in the macOS Keychain. Tokenomics never extracts or copies your tokens anywhere else. The specifics are itemized on the Privacy page.

How do I uninstall Tokenomics?

Quit the app, then drag Tokenomics from your Applications folder to the Trash. What's left behind is small: your settings and the usage numbers the widgets read. For a completely clean removal you can delete Tokenomics' entry from ~/Library/Preferences, but it's harmless to leave. To remove the browser extension, right-click its toolbar icon and choose Remove; that deletes everything it saved.

The browser extension

What does the browser extension have access to?

Only the AI sites it works with: Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Midjourney, ElevenLabs, Grok, Perplexity and Leonardo. It doesn't ask for access to all websites. On those sites it reads your own usage and limit numbers, using the sign-in you already have. It saves your settings and latest numbers in your browser's extension storage on your device, and for ElevenLabs and Leonardo it also saves the access token the site gives your browser. Numbers refresh every few minutes.

Does it start reading as soon as I install it?

No. On install it shows a short consent screen that explains what it reads and stores. Nothing is read until you click Agree and turn on. To turn it off later, open the extension's settings and click Stop reading and delete my data. That deletes everything it saved, and it stays off until you agree again.

Why does it need access to the AI sites?

Each site shows your usage in its own account pages, with no shared way to get it. To show it in the popup, the extension asks that site for your numbers, the same way the site's own pages do. That is the only reason it has access to those sites. It doesn't touch any others.

Why does Chrome say it can “communicate with cooperating native applications”?

That's the permission the extension uses to hand your usage numbers to the Tokenomics Mac app, if you have it installed. It stays on your computer, and your sign-in tokens are never sent over it. Without the Mac app, nothing happens.

Does it see my chats or prompts?

No. The extension reads usage numbers, not conversations, and it doesn't store your prompts or chats. On ChatGPT it notes the time and model of each message you send, to estimate usage when exact numbers aren't available. It never keeps what you wrote. Details are on the privacy page.

Is any of my data sent to Tokenomics?

No. There's no analytics or telemetry in the extension. It talks only to the AI sites you use, plus the Mac app on your own computer if you have it. The privacy page lists exactly what it connects to.

Are the numbers exact?

Not always. Some sites report your usage directly. Others don't, and Tokenomics can only estimate. When a reading is an estimate, the popup shows an “estimated” label next to the plan.

Does the extension work without the Mac app?

Yes. It works on its own in your browser. The Mac app adds menu bar rings and desktop widgets, and brings in Claude Code, Codex CLI, Gemini CLI, GitHub Copilot and Cursor. If you have both, they sync.

Is Tokenomics affiliated with these AI services?

No. Tokenomics is independent and unofficial. It uses your own signed-in session to read your own usage. Each service has its own terms; please read them. Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Midjourney, ElevenLabs, Grok, Perplexity, Leonardo and related names are the property of their owners and are used here only to say which services Tokenomics works with.
